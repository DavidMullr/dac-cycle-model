Reference simulations

Each 48-hour run starts with a clean, dry bed at a fixed resin capacity. Feed is 25 °C, 65% RH and 420 ppm CO₂. The cycle uses 48 min of adsorption, 2 min of evacuation and 83 min of electrical regeneration at a 91.5 °C heater setpoint and 16 kPa absolute vacuum target. Air returns once the hottest bed cell and the wall are both at or below 55 °C.

The model scales the dry and humid CO₂ equilibrium capacities. It holds water uptake parameters and adsorption kinetics fixed. Estimates between reference runs use linear interpolation. Steam regeneration, other resins and seasonal feeds need their own reference runs.

What 180 cycles tells us

Hunt and colleagues measured 68.8% of fresh Lewatit capacity after 180 cycles with regeneration at 80 °C and 10 kPa absolute. The linear scenario loses an equal amount of capacity per cycle. The exponential scenario loses an equal fraction of the remaining capacity. Both use that published endpoint. Their spread only shows the effect of choosing a curve. Hunt et al. (2024)

Our bed runs at different conditions, so its loss rate and service life still require measurements. Cycle 180 marks the end of the experiment. A replacement decision can use measured capture, electricity and resin cost. See the aging equations and assumptions.
