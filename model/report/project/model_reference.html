Domain and state variables

In the reviewed electrical case the spatial engine (screening/mof_screening/spatial.py) resolves the transverse thermal thickness of a symmetric bed, with parallel gas paths. It is not an axial breakthrough model. In the reported case the full bed is 16 mm thick and the half-domain has 16 cells. Gas and solid temperatures, gas species inventories and adsorbate storage are local states. Heater, wall and cumulative boundary meters complete the state vector.

The gas species are CO₂, H₂O, N₂ and O₂. Adsorbate storage can have extra CO₂ reservoirs or sites; a fixed species mapping sums their gas-phase contributions. All loadings refer to the complete dry shaped material. The full-device dry mass is split once for the half-domain. Reported device totals apply the symmetry factor once.

L1/2=L/2,Δx=L/(2N),ms,1/2=ms,device/2. L_{1/2}=L/2,\qquad \Delta x=L/(2N),\qquad m_{s,1/2}=m_{s,\mathrm{device}}/2.

For cell gas volume Vg,jV_{g,j} and gas temperature Tg,jT_{g,j}, ideal-gas closure gives

Pj=RTg,jVg,j∑ini,j,yi,j=ni,j∑knk,j,pi,j=yi,jPj. P_j=\frac{RT_{g,j}}{V_{g,j}}\sum_i n_{i,j},\qquad y_{i,j}=\frac{n_{i,j}}{\sum_k n_{k,j}},\qquad p_{i,j}=y_{i,j}P_j.

Feed moisture is set by relative humidity at the ambient temperature, not at the regeneration setpoint:

yw,in=RHpsat(Ta)Pa,yc,in=(1−yw,in)zc,dry. y_{w,\mathrm{in}}=\frac{\mathrm{RH}\,p_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_a)}{P_a},\qquad y_{c,\mathrm{in}}=(1-y_{w,\mathrm{in}})z_{c,\mathrm{dry}}.

The implementation checks the domain of its saturation correlation. A declared hot-state humidity diagnostic is not a new high-temperature water-property fit.

Geometry, inventory and initial conditions

The case specifies dry shaped mass msm_s, full thickness LL, face area AA and bed void fraction ε\varepsilon. The runtime derives the volume and densities from these four inputs. The gas volume includes the interparticle void and the specified dead volume.

Vb=AL,ρb=ms/Vb,ρp=ρb/(1−ε),Vg=εVb+Vdead. V_b=AL,\qquad \rho_b=m_s/V_b,\qquad \rho_p=\rho_b/(1-\varepsilon),\qquad V_g=\varepsilon V_b+V_{\mathrm{dead}}.

In the reviewed 10 kg case, A=1.73611A=1.73611 m² and L=0.016L=0.016 m, giving Vb=0.02778V_b=0.02778 m³, ρb=360\rho_b=360 kg/m³ and ρp=600\rho_p=600 kg/m³. With ε=0.40\varepsilon=0.40 and 0.0005 m³ of dead volume, the full-device gas volume is 0.01161 m³. Each of the 16 equal half-bed cells has 0.3125 kg dry material and is 0.5 mm thick. The hydraulic path in the pressure-loss estimate is the full 16 mm. The conduction path from the wall to the centre plane is 8 mm.

clean_dry initializes all adsorbed loadings to zero. The gas spaces still contain the chosen ambient mixture at the initial pressure and temperature. equilibrated_with_first_weather initializes the solid at the material’s equilibrium with that mixture. Subsequent phases inherit the preceding inventories and temperatures. Restarting every adsorption phase with an empty bed would give a different production estimate.

Weather and local partial pressures

For a fixed case, the same forcing is applied across the horizon. With Pully weather, the model reads the normalized hourly observations in chronological order. WeatherWindow.at selects the hourly interval for the elapsed simulation time. The feed water mole fraction uses Buck saturation pressure at the ambient temperature. The remaining dry fraction contains the specified CO₂ and the balance of N₂ and O₂ in the proportions 0.7905 and 0.2095.

For a check on units, the reviewed humid feed at 25 °C, 100,000 Pa and 65% RH gives pw,in=2059.55p_{w,\mathrm{in}}=2059.55 Pa. At 420 ppm CO₂ on a dry basis, the wet-feed CO₂ partial pressure is 100000(1−0.02059545)(420×10−6)=41.13100000(1-0.02059545)(420\times10^{-6})=41.13 Pa. Inside each cell, the solver uses its current gas temperature to calculate pip_i and evaluates the material law at its current solid temperature. In particular, local water activity aw=pw/psat(Ts)a_w=p_w/p_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_s) can change during regeneration even at constant inlet RH. The selected material specifies the local saturation correlation.

The following functions construct the geometry, weather and initial state: runtime.derived_geometry, geometry.build_bare_geometry, weather.WeatherHour, spatial.build_model and spatial.initial_state.

Adsorption and component balance

The material adapter supplies equilibrium loading qa*q_a^*, rates kak_a, storage energy and its derivatives. Each storage state follows a linear driving force law:

q̇a,j=ka,j(qa,j*−qa,j). \dot q_{a,j}=k_{a,j}(q^*_{a,j}-q_{a,j}).

For a species-to-storage map SiaS_{ia}, the local gas balance is

ṅi,j=ṅi,jin−ṅi,jout+ṅi,jleak−ms,j∑aSiaq̇a,j. \dot n_{i,j}=\dot n_{i,j}^{\mathrm{in}}-\dot n_{i,j}^{\mathrm{out}} +\dot n_{i,j}^{\mathrm{leak}}-m_{s,j}\sum_a S_{ia}\dot q_{a,j}.

The material package determines which equilibrium expression is used. The supported families include independent Langmuir/Sips laws, reference-curve temperature shifts, split-rate reservoirs with shared thermodynamics, explicit two-site CO₂ storage and the Lewatit water-dependent treatment. These families are not interchangeable descriptions of the same specimen.

A split-rate empirical response uses two storage reservoirs with nonnegative fractions that sum to one:

qc=qf+qs,qf*=fqc*,qs*=(1−f)qc*,0≤f≤1. q_c=q_f+q_s,\qquad q_f^*=f q_c^*,\qquad q_s^*=(1-f)q_c^*,\qquad 0\le f\le1.

It can represent more than one protocol timescale without establishing two independently measured thermodynamic sites. Stored-water scenarios make the CO₂ target depend on current adsorbed water. Their humid coupling and transfer assumptions remain part of the material evidence. An unwrapped dry_only package has exactly zero water storage, rate and adsorption energy and requires exactly zero feed RH.

The material records identify pressure and temperature support separately from the numerical extension of a curve. Named extrapolation scenarios retain the evidence limits. The temperature registry distinguishes a scenario guard from a demonstrated physical material limit.

Langmuir, Sips and reference-curve models

In the independent family, qrq_r at partial pressure prp_r anchors CO₂ affinity at TrT_r. Water has a Sips exponent ν\nu and a half-loading affinity. The optional penalty α\alpha links the two equilibrium targets. Nitrogen and oxygen follow Henry laws in this family.

bc,r=qrpr(qs,c−qr),qw*=qs,w(bwpw)ν1+(bwpw)ν,qc*=qs,cbcpc(1+bcpc)(1+αqw*). b_{c,r}=\frac{q_r}{p_r(q_{s,c}-q_r)},\quad q_w^*=q_{s,w}\frac{(b_wp_w)^\nu}{1+(b_wp_w)^\nu},\quad q_c^*=\frac{q_{s,c}b_cp_c}{(1+b_cp_c)(1+\alpha q_w^*)}.

With positive reference heat magnitude Qi,rQ_{i,r} and ΔCp,i=Cp,g,i−Cp,a,i\Delta C_{p,i}=C_{p,g,i}-C_{p,a,i} in J/(mol K), the affinity factor used for these species is

ln⁡bi(T)bi(Tr)=Qi,rR(1T−1Tr)+ΔCp,iR[1−TrT−lnTTr]. \ln\frac{b_i(T)}{b_i(T_r)} =\frac{Q_{i,r}}{R}\left(\frac1T-\frac1{T_r}\right) +\frac{\Delta C_{p,i}}{R}\left[1-\frac{T_r}{T}-\ln\frac{T}{T_r}\right].

For a measured reference curve at TmT_m, the CO₂ target instead comes from mapping the current pressure back to the reference temperature:

pmap=pcexp⁡[QcR(1Ts−1Tm)],qc*=qcurve(pmap). p_{\mathrm{map}}=p_c\exp\left[\frac{Q_c}{R}\left(\frac1{T_s}-\frac1{T_m}\right)\right], \qquad q_c^*=q_{\mathrm{curve}}(p_{\mathrm{map}}).

DryActivityShiftReferenceEvaluator interpolates loading in log pressure with a shape-preserving piecewise cubic (PCHIP). Below the first positive point it uses a straight line to the origin. Above the final point it holds the final loading. The material record identifies the measured range and the evidence for temperature transfer. COF-1000 partitions this target between two reservoirs. Shaped CALF-20 instead evaluates each site aa at c=pc/(RTs)c=p_c/(RT_s):

ba(Ts)=b0,aexp⁡[−ua/(RTs)],qa*=qs,aba(Ts)c1+ba(Ts)c,qc=q1+q2. b_a(T_s)=b_{0,a}\exp[-u_a/(RT_s)],\qquad q_a^*=q_{s,a}\frac{b_a(T_s)c}{1+b_a(T_s)c},\qquad q_c=q_1+q_2 .

Here uau_a is the signed energy parameter stored for that site. In the native shaped CALF-20 record, the separate water curve is evaluated at a temperature-shifted reference activity. The exploratory humid record declares its own water treatment. Numerical interpolation between observations does not establish hot, dilute or humid behavior beyond their support.

How humidity changes the reviewed Lewatit result

In the matched 48-hour Lewatit runs the gas supplies local pcp_c and pwp_w. At the solid temperature the Young et al. adapter evaluates a Guggenheim–Anderson–de Boer (GAB) water target and a weighted average of two Toth CO₂ targets (Young et al. 2021). With aw=pw/psat(Ts)a_w=p_w/p_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_s), monolayer loading qmq_m, and temperature-dependent GAB coefficients C,KC,K,

qw*=qmCKaw(1−Kaw)[1+(C−1)Kaw]. q_w^*=\frac{q_m C K a_w} {(1-Ka_w)[1+(C-1)Ka_w]} .

For each of the dry and wet CO₂ branches,

qb(T,pc)=q∞,b(T)bb(T)pc[1+(bb(T)pc)τb(T)]1/τb(T). q_b(T,p_c)=q_{\infty,b}(T) \frac{b_b(T)p_c}{[1+(b_b(T)p_c)^{\tau_b(T)}]^{1/\tau_b(T)}} .

The implemented parameter convention is bb=b0,bexp⁡[Q0,b/(RT)]b_b=b_{0,b}\exp[Q_{0,b}/(RT)], q∞,b=q∞,0,bexp⁡[χb(1−T/Tr)]q_{\infty,b}=q_{\infty,0,b}\exp[\chi_b(1-T/T_r)] and τb=τ0,b+αb(1−Tr/T)\tau_b=\tau_{0,b}+\alpha_b(1-T_r/T). young2021.py gives the separate dry and wet coefficients and the GAB temperature functions. The fraction assigned to wet sites depends on the current stored water qwq_w, with qcrit=1.532q_{\mathrm{crit}}=1.532 mol/kg. It is zero at qw=0q_w=0:

w=exp⁡(−qcrit/qw)(qw>0),qc*=(1−w)qdry+wqwet. w=\exp(-q_{\mathrm{crit}}/q_w)\quad(q_w>0),\qquad q_c^*=(1-w)q_{\mathrm{dry}}+w q_{\mathrm{wet}}.

Water therefore affects CO₂ after it has loaded onto the material. An instantaneous change in inlet RH does not immediately impose a new wet-site fraction. Both loadings approach their targets with the applicable directional rates. The matched exports use the advisory-temperature material with the named BDF trial-recovery policy. Its identifier contains dry_bdf_recovery in both exports. Feed humidity is defined in case.weather. The material’s measured coupled temperature range is 25–70 °C. At the 116 °C regeneration request the model extrapolates the equations. The advisory implementation uses IAPWS for local saturation pressure, while the original strict Young implementation uses Buck. The saved RH trace has a separate diagnostic convention described below.

Purolite A110 under humid air

The A110 adapter uses the commercial free-base resin on a dry bead mass basis. Piscina et al. measured CO₂ uptake on water-presaturated resin at 25 °C. The four digitized curves represent dry air and 30%, 55% and 80% RH. Within each curve the model interpolates loading in log CO₂ pressure. The first humid points lie at 52–65 Pa. At 420 ppm in 100 kPa air, the CO₂ partial pressure is only about 42 Pa, so all three humid curves use the dry Toth shape below their first point, matched to that point. Above the last pressure the loading stays constant. (Piscina et al. 2026; Low et al. 2023)

During a cycle the water loading approaches a GAB equilibrium at the local gas humidity. Inverting that relation at the stored water loading gives the humidity used to interpolate between CO₂ curves. A newly exposed dry bead therefore takes time to reach the humid response. Above 80% the CO₂ target uses the last curve, while the water target continues to saturation. The GAB shape coefficients use the nearest fitted temperature outside 15–35 °C.

For temperature TT, the pressure read on the 25 °C isotherm is pCO2exp⁡[(70100/R)(1/T−1/298.15)]p_{\mathrm{CO_2}}\exp[(70100/R)(1/T-1/298.15)], with heat in J/mol and temperatures in K. A dry CO₂ rate fit supplies the reference linear driving force coefficient. The rate scales with temperature between 20 and 70 °C and with relative diffusivity at the bead humidity. The water rate uses diffusivity at the gas humidity. Desorption uses the same coefficients as adsorption. Hot wet release, humidity-induced swelling and changes in packed-bed resistance need separate measurements. The 120 °C scenario guard does not establish a material operating limit. (Low et al. 2025)

Lewatit bed aging

The Bed aging estimate uses four 48-hour electrical runs at fixed resin conditions. Each multiplies the dry and wet Toth saturation capacities by the same retained fraction rr. The water isotherm, adsorption and desorption rate coefficients, geometry and equipment retain their fresh-bed values. Each run starts with a clean, dry 10 kg bed and one pump. Between the simulated capacities (68.8%, 75%, 90% and 100%) the app interpolates each reported metric separately. A measured aged/fresh capacity ratio obtained under the same test conditions can be entered directly.

For a cycle count nn from 0 to 180, the two scenarios use rlinear=1−0.312n/180r_{\mathrm{linear}}=1-0.312n/180 and rexp=0.688n/180r_{\mathrm{exp}}=0.688^{n/180}. Hunt et al. measured a 31.2% loss at 180 cycles with regeneration at 80 °C and 10 kPa absolute. Our one-pump case uses a 91.5 °C heater setpoint and a 16 kPa vacuum target. Neither curve predicts damage from the simulated temperature or oxygen history. The interface reports the last complete cycle in kg/day and startup-inclusive 48-hour electricity in kWh/kg. It gives estimates only within the simulated capacity range. (Hunt et al. 2024) Reference runs

Other stored-water scenarios

For the COFs, MOF-808 variants and PEI/MIL variants that specify a chemical-potential shift, the wrapper evaluates the dry CO₂ law with an effective pressure. With x=qw/qnormx=q_w/q_{\mathrm{norm}}, the record’s coefficients aka_k and temperature exponent η\eta give

pc,eff=pcexp⁡[(Tr/Ts)η∑k≥1akxk]. p_{c,\mathrm{eff}}=p_c\exp\left[(T_r/T_s)^\eta\sum_{k\ge1} a_k x^k\right].

The TEPA proxy instead adds a bounded loading qadd[1−exp⁡(−xm)]b(Ts)pc/[1+b(Ts)pc]q_{\mathrm{add}}[1-\exp(-x^m)]\,b(T_s)p_c/[1+b(T_s)p_c] to the dry target. For independent-water candidates such as HOB/PVDF or mmen, water still adds inventory, sorption heat and pump load while the direct CO₂ target follows the dry law. Depending on the record, the water target comes from a Sips anchor, a line through one measured node, a PCHIP curve, a temperature/activity table or the native water relation. The package identifies which coefficients were measured, fitted, transferred or assumed.

In each reservoir the model uses the adsorption coefficient when qa*≥qaq_a^*\ge q_a and the desorption coefficient when qa*<qaq_a^*<q_a. Thus a cell can still adsorb during a nominal desorption phase if its local target exceeds its current loading. The gas sees the sum of rates for all reservoirs assigned to its species.

Energy and heat transfer

The gas uses constant species heat capacities and an explicit internal-energy/enthalpy reference. Stored energy includes gas, dry solid, adsorbed material, wall and heater contributions. Loading-energy derivatives are supplied by the material law, avoiding a second independent addition of adsorption heat.

The solid energy derivative has the general form

dUs(Ts,𝒒)dt=(∂Us∂Ts)𝒒Ṫs+∑a(∂Us∂qa)Tsq̇a. \frac{dU_s(T_s,\boldsymbol q)}{dt} =\left(\frac{\partial U_s}{\partial T_s}\right)_{\boldsymbol q}\dot T_s +\sum_a\left(\frac{\partial U_s}{\partial q_a}\right)_{T_s}\dot q_a.

Let Ės\dot E_s include heat received and the internal energy carried by gas entering storage. Subtracting the energy stored through loading changes gives the solid temperature rate.

Ṫs=Ės−∑aUqaq̇aUT. \dot T_s=\frac{\dot E_s-\sum_a U_{q_a}\dot q_a}{U_T}.

The spatial case uses an assumed gas–solid interfacial area of 2,400 m² per m³ of bed. This stays fixed when particle diameter changes. A geometry or particle-size sensitivity must review that assumption. Finite gas-to-solid heat exchange uses

Q̇gs,j=hgsAgs,j(Tg,j−Ts,j). \dot Q_{gs,j}=h_{gs}A_{gs,j}(T_{g,j}-T_{s,j}).

Neighboring solid cells exchange heat through the specified effective conductivity:

Q̇j→j+1=keffAΔx(Ts,j−Ts,j+1). \dot Q_{j\rightarrow j+1}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{eff}}A}{\Delta x} (T_{s,j}-T_{s,j+1}).

There is no extra transverse gas-conduction term on top of that effective bulk conductivity. The symmetry plane has zero normal heat flux. Wall/heater heat capacities, transfer conductances, ambient losses and bounded actuator requests determine the boundary behavior. Heater power is an electrical input, not an imposed uniform bed temperature. A resistance wire inside a real bed needs its own transfer representation and temperature measurements.

Balances actually integrated in a spatial cell

Let FjF_j be feed, BjB_j leakage and WjW_j withdrawal in mol/s for one half-domain cell. Let ϕj=hgsAgs,j(Tg,j−Ts,j)\phi_j=h_{gs}A_{gs,j}(T_{g,j}-T_{s,j}) be heat flowing into the solid. The spatial engine integrates the gas temperature together with the species inventories. For inlet and leak gas at TaT_a its gas temperature equation is

njC‾v,jṪg,j=(FjC‾p,F+BjC‾p,B)(Ta−Tg,j)+RTg,j(Fj+Bj−Wj)−ϕj. n_j\bar C_{v,j}\dot T_{g,j} =(F_j\bar C_{p,F}+B_j\bar C_{p,B})(T_a-T_{g,j}) +RT_{g,j}(F_j+B_j-W_j)-\phi_j .

Here nj=∑ini,jn_j=\sum_i n_{i,j} and the barred heat capacities are mole-fraction weighted. Uptake already removes gas internal energy with the transferred moles. With reference temperature TrT_r the code uses uig=(Cp,i−R)(Tg−Tr)u_i^g=(C_{p,i}-R)(T_g-T_r) and hig=Cp,i(Tg−Tr)+RTrh_i^g=C_{p,i}(T_g-T_r)+RT_r. Thus hig−uig=RTgh_i^g-u_i^g=RT_g at the same temperature. The cell’s conservative solid equation is

mj(cp,s+∂ea∂Ts)Ṫs,j=Q̇cond,j+ϕj+mj∑aq̇a,j[ui(a)g(Tg,j)−∂ea∂qa]. m_j\left(c_{p,s}+\frac{\partial e_a}{\partial T_s}\right)\dot T_{s,j} =\dot Q_{\mathrm{cond},j}+\phi_j+ m_j\sum_a\dot q_{a,j} \left[u^g_{i(a)}(T_{g,j})-\frac{\partial e_a}{\partial q_a}\right].

ea(Ts,𝒒)e_a(T_s,\boldsymbol q) is stored adsorbate energy in J per kg dry shaped material. The difference in brackets is the energy transferred per mole taken up. Using both this term and a separately added heat of adsorption would count the same transfer twice. This is why a new material law must supply its caloric terms as well as its isotherm.

Wall, heater and cooling boundary

Cell 1 lies at the symmetry plane. The last cell touches the wall through a contact resistance and half of the boundary cell’s conduction resistance. For N>1N>1,

Gss=keffA/Δx,Rws=(hcontactA)−1+Δx/(2keffA),Q̇ws=(Tw−Ts,N)/Rws. G_{ss}=k_{\mathrm{eff}}A/\Delta x,\qquad R_{ws}=(h_{\mathrm{contact}}A)^{-1}+\Delta x/(2k_{\mathrm{eff}}A),\qquad \dot Q_{ws}=(T_w-T_{s,N})/R_{ws}.

For a one-cell spatial calculation the code uses the full half-thickness in the wall conduction resistance. It still has distinct gas, solid, wall and heater temperatures. For the reviewed electric heater, the two boundary-node balances, written with half-device powers and capacities, are

ChṪh=Ph−Ghw(Th−Tw),CwṪw=Ghw(Th−Tw)−Q̇ws−UlossA(Tw−Ta)−Q̇wall,cool. C_h\dot T_h=P_h-G_{hw}(T_h-T_w),\qquad C_w\dot T_w=G_{hw}(T_h-T_w)-\dot Q_{ws}-U_{\mathrm{loss}}A(T_w-T_a)-\dot Q_{\mathrm{wall},cool}.

The coldest solid cell supplies the spatial feedback temperature. The heater command is capped at rated power and tapers as the heater approaches its own temperature ceiling. Wall cooling also requires a temperature difference, finite wall conductance and capacity left over after the pump-inlet cooler. The hottest bed cell is checked against any active material guard. Control temperature, local peak and requested setpoint therefore refer to three different quantities.

Well-mixed companion

The lumped engine carries one temperature TT for gas, sorbent, represented metal and heater. Pressure follows nRT/VgnRT/V_g. Adsorption uses a fixed-total-moles control policy and limits positive uptake demand by available feed for each species. The common material interface still calculates the target and directional rates. The lumped first law can be written

CeffṪ=Q̇h−Q̇cool−Q̇loss+Ḣin−Ḣout−∑iuigṅi−ms∑a∂ea∂qaq̇a, C_{\mathrm{eff}}\dot T=\dot Q_h-\dot Q_{\mathrm{cool}}-\dot Q_{\mathrm{loss}} +\dot H_{\mathrm{in}}-\dot H_{\mathrm{out}} -\sum_i u_i^g\dot n_i - m_s\sum_a \frac{\partial e_a}{\partial q_a}\dot q_a,

with Ceff=ms(cp,s+∂ea/∂T)+Cmetal+Ch+∑iniCv,iC_{\mathrm{eff}}=m_s(c_{p,s}+\partial e_a/\partial T)+C_{\mathrm{metal}}+C_h+\sum_i n_i C_{v,i}. Heating and cooling act at the mixed temperature. Gas–solid exchange, transverse conduction and wall-to-bed temperature lags have no independent states here. A spatial mesh refinement and a spatial/lumped comparison consequently answer different questions.

The whole-device first-law check compares inventory change with integrated boundary enthalpy and heat:

rE=ΔU−∫(Ḣin−Ḣout+Q̇net)dt. r_E=\Delta U-\int(\dot H_{\mathrm{in}}-\dot H_{\mathrm{out}}+\dot Q_{\mathrm{net}})\,dt.

The reported relative residual uses gross boundary exchange, so cancellation between large inward and outward terms does not create a misleading denominator:

ϵE=|rE|max⁡(|ΔU|,Egross,1J). \epsilon_E=\frac{|r_E|}{\max(|\Delta U|,E_{\mathrm{gross}},1\ \mathrm J)}.

energy_balance.py retains the older net-exchange diagnostic alongside this value. The gross criterion is 10−610^{-6}; it is an accounting threshold, not a prediction-error estimate. The raw species balances and absolute residuals remain necessary evidence.

Airflow, vacuum and collection

For a packed-bed hydraulic estimate the model uses the Ergun relationship (Ergun 1952) with declared particle diameter dpd_p, porosity ε\varepsilon and superficial velocity uu:

ΔPL=150μ(1−ε)2ε3dp2u+1.75ρ(1−ε)ε3dpu|u|. \frac{\Delta P}{L}=150\frac{\mu(1-\varepsilon)^2}{\varepsilon^3d_p^2}u +1.75\frac{\rho(1-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^3d_p}u|u|.

The fan work follows the imposed flow and modeled pressure loss:

Pfan=QΔP/ηfan,Q=uA. P_{\mathrm{fan}}=Q\Delta P/\eta_{\mathrm{fan}},\qquad Q=uA.

This does not represent a measured fan/system intersection or distributor uniformity. Screens, filters, fittings and an internal heater may add pressure loss.

Vacuum withdrawal uses the retained KNF N922 50 Hz reference curve and the pressure-control policy. The spatial control observable uses the maximum local pressure. The saved spatial endpoint compares the lowest cell pressure at the end of desorption with 1.05 times the request. Inspect the maximum pressure and spread in the traces to assess the rest of the bed. An ultimate-vacuum rating does not imply useful flow at that pressure. The current model does not qualify delivery into a 3 bar absolute destination.

The ideal inlet cooler/knockout has zero pressure drop and no line holdup in the common case. Its cooling request is allocated first within the shared 2 kW cooling capacity; remaining capacity is available to the bed wall. Water condensate, water vapor in product and adsorption exhaust are separate boundary flows. They are not additional stored inventory.

From bed outlet to pump and product

The case translator selects one pump and the reference_fixed_inlet_speed interpretation. For the plotted reference-volume flow Qcurve(P)Q_{\mathrm{curve}}(P) in m³/s and profile reference pressure PrP_r, the assumed inlet speed and molar capacity are

S(P)=Qcurve(P)Pr/P,ṅcap=S(P)P/(RTinlet). S(P)=Q_{\mathrm{curve}}(P)P_r/P,\qquad \dot n_{\mathrm{cap}}=S(P)P/(RT_{\mathrm{inlet}}).

In spatial operation the throttle command is α=clip[(Pmax−Preq)/(500Pa),0,1]\alpha=\mathrm{clip}[(P_{\max}-P_{\mathrm{req}})/(500\ {\mathrm{Pa}}),0,1]. Capacity uses the minimum cell pressure as suction pressure. The outlet mixture is formed using the prescribed channel shares and gas enthalpies. The single pump’s withdrawal is then apportioned to those channels. In the lumped pump/conditioner path the opening uses the mixed pressure, a 2,000 Pa band and a 0.02 offset. An identical pressure request can therefore yield different evacuation times in the two representations.

The inlet conditioner cools only when needed, to To=min⁡(Tmix,328.15K)T_o=\min(T_{\mathrm{mix}},328.15\ {\mathrm{K}}). Take one mole of raw bed gas with water fraction ywy_w, at suction pressure PP. The water fraction in the conditioned vapour is z=min⁡[yw,psat(To)/P]z=\min[y_w,p_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_o)/P]. When liquid forms from a gas with a nonzero dry fraction, the remaining vapour fraction is γ=(1−yw)/(1−z)\gamma=(1-y_w)/(1-z). The liquid drain is 1−γ1-\gamma mol of water per mol of raw gas. All incoming CO₂ stays in the gas in this ideal knockout model.

The enthalpy difference between the raw inlet and the two outlet streams gives the cooling required per mole, qcondq_{\mathrm{cond}} (J/mol). The raw withdrawal ṅb\dot n_b must satisfy both the conditioned-gas capacity and the cooling budget. When both bounds apply and are finite,

ṅb=αmin⁡(ṅcapγ,Q̇cool,maxqcond),Q̇cond=ṅbqcond. \dot n_b=\alpha\min\!\left(\frac{\dot n_{\mathrm{cap}}}{\gamma}, \frac{\dot Q_{\mathrm{cool},max}}{q_{\mathrm{cond}}}\right),\qquad \dot Q_{\mathrm{cond}}=\dot n_b q_{\mathrm{cond}}.

Zero cooling duty removes the energy constraint. A closed valve or zero pump capacity gives zero withdrawal. The wall can use at most Q̇cool,max−Q̇cond\dot Q_{\mathrm{cool},max}-\dot Q_{\mathrm{cond}}. In the reviewed case the shared cooling capacity is 2 kW and the coefficient of performance is 3. Both duties enter cooling electricity once. Thermal duty in watts and electrical power in watts have separate meters.

In the code, airflow.airflow adds the configured minor loss Kminorρu2/2K_{\mathrm{minor}}\rho u^2/2 to the packed-bed drop. pump_rates.rate_at_pressure interprets the pump curve. spatial.cycle_rhs allocates withdrawal and lumped_port.pump_conditioner.evaluate_pump_conditioner closes the gas, liquid and cooling balances.

Adsorption and heated desorption last two hours each in the saved comparison. Evacuation lasts five minutes, with bounded cooling and passive repressurization. Product collection is active during desorption and cooling, including any collected product before the exact horizon ends.

Direct steam and the water balance

Select Direct steam injection to resolve the gas path from the steam inlet to the pump. The 1D finite volumes exchange gas species and enthalpy by upwind advection. Each internal face solves the viscous and inertial Ergun terms for signed velocity using its pressure gradient and available void. Liquid displaces gas in the equation of state. Equal half-area lanes represent the full device. Each axial cell exchanges heat with the shared wall, which removes the transverse temperature resolution of the reviewed electrical case. The steam supply delivers saturated vapour during desorption, limited by the specified flow. The ideal regulator tapers linearly within 5% of absolute supply pressure and closes at backpressure. The outlet pump uses its existing conditioned suction curve. Adsorption uses imposed air flow with the corresponding wet-bed fan duty.

In one cell let nvn_v denote gas-phase water, nln_l the free liquid, VgV_g the gas volume and qwq_w the material’s adsorbed water. Saturation uses the bead temperature TsT_s. With gas temperature TgT_g, phase transfer and drainage are

nv,sat=psat(Ts)VgRTg,rph=max⁡(nv−nv,sat,−nl)τph,rd=nlτd,ṅl=rph−rd. n_{v,\mathrm{sat}}=\frac{p_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_s)V_g}{RT_g},\qquad r_{\mathrm{ph}}=\frac{\max(n_v-n_{v,\mathrm{sat}},-n_l)}{\tau_{\mathrm{ph}}},\qquad r_d=\frac{n_l}{\tau_d},\qquad \dot n_l=r_{\mathrm{ph}}-r_d .

Positive rphr_{\mathrm{ph}} condenses vapour. Negative values evaporate available liquid. The gas loses rphr_{\mathrm{ph}} in addition to water adsorbed by the sorbent. The sorbent equilibrium law uses an explicit activity plateau near saturation. The actual water partial pressure drives condensation, including when the material’s fitted equilibrium law reaches its plateau. The liquid has bulk-water heat capacity and remains at the solid temperature. Excess liquid drains locally. Reaching the packed void volume stops the calculation.

The bed energy inventory includes gas internal energy, adsorbed-phase energy, free-liquid sensible energy and the solid, heater and wall. Incoming steam carries vapour enthalpy and drain water leaves with liquid enthalpy on the same reference. On the gas-internal-energy basis, the heat released to the solid and film is rph[uv(Tg)−hl(Ts)]r_{\mathrm{ph}}[u_v(T_g)-h_l(T_s)]. Adsorption heat is calculated from the change in adsorbed inventory. Saturation follows IAPWS. Vapour and liquid enthalpies use the project’s constant-heat-capacity closure anchored at 100 °C. Only the indirect jacket applies heat directly to the wall. For direct injection the generator input is ∫ṅsteam(hsteam−hfeedwater)/ηdt\int\dot n_{\mathrm{steam}}(h_{\mathrm{steam}}-h_{\mathrm{feedwater}})/\eta\,dt. Choosing an electric generator includes this input in electricity once. External heat is metered separately. The pump-inlet knockout and its cooling duty are downstream of the bed drain.

Interpreting the film model. The phase-transfer time, drain time, gas-path geometry, valve law and sorption plateau are screening assumptions. The model resolves bulk liquid holdup. Pore-scale capillary condensation, liquid migration between cells, droplet entrainment, dissolved CO₂, sorbent degradation and two-phase flooding hydraulics require additional closures and measurements. Inspect peak local pw/psat(Ts)p_w/p_{\mathrm{sat}}(T_s), drainage and steam breakthrough. Large transient supersaturation makes the phase-transfer assumption especially consequential. Vary both time constants and the plateau, refine cell size and time tolerance, and compare with measured axial temperatures, bed pressures, steam inlet, separate bed and knockout drain weights, outlet humidity and net CO₂. Experiments on amine-functionalized monoliths and a dynamic condensation study establish useful trends for this comparison. Transferring their times or rates to Lewatit beads requires independent evidence. Direct-heating experiments and dynamic condensation study.

Numerical solution and qualification

The default spatial solver is SciPy BDF, with state-scaled absolute tolerances and a bounded numerical Jacobian over physical states. Independent cumulative meters have no feedback columns. A narrow dry-water trial-recovery policy is explicit in the relevant package. Completed mmen and TAPA rate sensitivities used the separately included branch-aware Jacobian, Radau solver and exact cell cache. Their original source hashes and numerical policies remain attached to the results; this extension is not presented as a new default for every material.

The primary cases use relative tolerance 10−710^{-7} and a maximum time step of 10 s. Solver success, finite sampled states, mass/energy closure, cycle repeatability, mesh independence and physical validation are separate checks. A completed integration establishes only that the requested numerical calculation finished. This release does not claim CSS, a mesh-qualified material ranking, validated hot humid behavior or device-level uncertainty bounds.

Integration and evidence to inspect

The spatial solver integrates temperatures, gas moles, adsorbed loadings and boundary meters together. Its absolute tolerances differ by state: 10−510^{-5} K for temperatures, 10−910^{-9} mol/kg for loadings and 10−1010^{-10} mol for cell gas inventories. max_step_s caps internal solver steps; samples_per_phase controls the reported trace resolution. Increasing the sample count alone therefore cannot demonstrate time-step or mesh convergence.

The lumped weather driver ends each integration segment at the next applicable phase, weather or horizon boundary and carries forward the accepted state. When diagnosing either engine, start with horizon and checks, then read endpoint, temperature_policy, numerical_reliability and the material’s provenance. A small conservation residual tests the bookkeeping. A mesh study requires reruns with the same physical inputs at different cell counts and tolerances. Physical validation requires independent measurements appropriate to the material, geometry and operating conditions.

The project map names the functions that run these equations and the result files they produce.

The 3D setup replays saved traces and phase-end fields. It holds the last saved field between boundaries. It does not resolve a new flow field or interpolate an axial adsorption front. Its equipment sizes and pipe arrangement are illustrative.
