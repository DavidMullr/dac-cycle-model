Lewatit under Lausanne weather

Download the four-page report (PDF).

A 10 kg Lewatit VP OC 1065 bed with one vacuum pump and electrical heating collected 5.27 to 6.12 kg of CO₂ over 48 hours across four Pully weather records. This is the cycle we would test first. The design objective is collected CO₂ per day within a CHF 24,000 incremental equipment budget. The figures below are simulations of the full bed. Installed cost still needs supplier quotes.

We kept the selected cycle settings fixed across seasons. The bed adsorbs for 48 min, evacuates for 2 min, then heats for 83 min at a 91.5 °C setpoint and a 16 kPa absolute pressure target. The pump-inlet knockout targets 35 °C. Air returns once the wall and hottest bed cell have both cooled to 55 °C. Cooling took about 11 min on average in each weather window.

Each run starts with a clean, dry bed at 420 ppm inlet CO₂. For the four weather runs, temperature, humidity and pressure follow 48 hourly observations at Pully. The table gives the mean inlet temperature and RH. Throughout this report, daily capture means all CO₂ collected in the first 48 hours divided by two, including startup and any partial final cycle. Electricity is the total metered over those same hours divided by the product collected. Four Pully runs · Fixed-inlet control

January collected 0.85 kg more than July over two days. July nevertheless used the least electricity per kilogram. Each weather run completed 19 cycles. The four windows are examples from 2023. Seasonal or annual production estimates would need longer weather records. The coupled Lewatit uptake data cover 25–70 °C, so both the colder inlets and hot regeneration require extrapolation.

Why we chose this cycle

We selected the timings at a fixed 25 °C and 65% RH before applying the weather records. A close alternative with 50 min adsorption, 90 s evacuation and 85 min heating gave a slightly higher rate in its last complete cycle (2.817 versus 2.805 kg/day). Over the full 48 hours it collected 5.42 kg, compared with 5.52 kg for the selected cycle. One-pump timing runs

The material screen asks which sorbent collects more CO₂ under one common operating schedule. All eleven rows use 10 kg of dry shaped material, a clean, dry start, and a fixed inlet at 25 °C, 65% RH, 100 kPa and 420 ppm dry CO₂. The common bed is 16 mm thick, with 1.736 m² face area, 0.40 void fraction, 1.5 mm particles and 0.18 m³/s airflow. The electrical heater and combined cooling each have 2 kW available. Sixteen cells resolve the half-thickness of the bed. Electricity includes the heater, fan, vacuum pump and cooling. Downstream compression and product treatment fall outside this boundary.

These figures compare the material models at the stated conditions. Several packages extrapolate hot, humid uptake or use rate coefficients measured under other conditions. For example, the Purolite model uses humidity-dependent CO₂ measurements at 25 °C and extrapolates to hot regeneration. We use 116 °C for the material screen and 91.5 °C for the Pully weather runs. What fits in one cartridge?

The baseline gives every resin the same dry mass. In a separate 27.78 L cartridge comparison, the dry packed densities used in the model give charges of 11.78 kg Purolite and 13.22 kg Lewatit. At 75% RH and a 100 °C heater setpoint, those charges collected 2.19 and 3.27 kg respectively in 48 hours. For procurement, the relevant measurement is how much dry resin our cartridge actually holds. Equal-volume resin runs

Humidity and regeneration sensitivity

We changed one input at a time from the common material baseline. With Lewatit, changing only the feed from 65% RH to dry air reduced collection from 2.570 to 1.186 kg/day and raised electricity use from 5.47 to 7.41 kWh/kg. This comparison tests the coupled water and CO₂ adsorption law under fixed conditions. Temperature, RH and pressure vary together in the Pully runs.

For the rate test we set the CO₂ desorption coefficient to 0.001 s⁻¹ and kept the adsorption and water rates fixed. Five materials completed the 48 hours. Each row compares a material with its own baseline.

Steam, electricity and cycle timing

For Lewatit at 25 °C and 65% RH we also tested different regeneration systems. The first row below is the fixed-inlet control for the Pully study. All rows use the full 48-hour collection metric.

Regeneration system Adsorption / heating (min) CO₂ in 48 h (kg) Electricity (kWh/kg CO₂)
Electrical, one pump 48 / 83 5.52 7.16
Direct steam, one pump 28 / 40 5.69 19.03
Electrical, two pumps 50 / 70 6.12 7.44

The one-pump steam case collected 0.17 kg more in two days than the one-pump electrical case. Producing the steam electrically raised electricity use to 19.03 kWh/kg. The steam case injected up to 10 kg/h and follows gas through cells in series. The electrical model uses parallel gas paths. Phase times also differ, so this comparison describes the complete operating choices. The fixed-weather steam run reused repeating cycles. Steam and electrical timing runs

How aging changes capture

We repeated the one-pump electrical cycle used in the weather study at a fixed 25 °C and 65% RH. Each run starts with a clean, dry bed at the stated fraction of fresh CO₂ capacity. Water uptake parameters and rate coefficients stay fixed.

Lewatit capacity retained CO₂ in 48 h (kg) Electricity (kWh/kg CO₂)
100% (fresh control) 5.52 7.16
90% 5.30 7.45
75% 4.88 8.09
68.8% 4.66 8.43

A 31.2% loss of equilibrium capacity reduced the CO₂ collected in two days by about 16%. The 68.8% capacity comes from Hunt and colleagues’ measurements after 180 cycles at 80 °C and 10 kPa absolute. The team’s 91.5 °C cycle has a different exposure history. A replacement interval would require uptake and operating-cost measurements on our own bed. Four fixed-capacity runs · Hunt et al.

Measurements for the small prototype

Lewatit is our first candidate for a prototype with resistance wire and a KNF N922 vacuum pump. The first test should measure bed pressure drop and resin temperature near the wire and in the coldest region. Record the hottest temperature when air returns. Measure product flow, CO₂ and moisture through a complete cycle. Weigh the condensate and meter electricity over the same interval. These measurements tie daily capture and electricity use to a physical bed.

The delivery target is approximately 1% CO₂ at at least 3 bar absolute. The concentration basis, required flow and pump throughput at simultaneous suction and discharge pressures still need confirmation. Collected CO₂ describes the product at the model boundary. Net atmospheric removal would also require downstream energy and life-cycle accounting. Pump reference
